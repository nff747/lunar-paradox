# Reference comparison — current live 3D build

The supplied JPEGs are visual targets; none is used as a scene backdrop. The
following comparison does not claim pixel equivalence or client acceptance.
The three platforms remain independent.

## Syzygy

The reference has a large near-circular sphere, a golden left limb with an
asymmetric white solar hotspot, charcoal/violet right crust, a jagged central
tear, diverse ejected rock, and bold two-line text crossing the composition.
Its small HUD is widely spaced, its pill lies below the sphere, and the light
locally obscures lettering. The build preserves this hierarchy, local type/light
occlusion, live cratered geometry, initially joined state, animated cohesive split
and instanced fragments. Mission now enters the actual rift with projected
selection points rather than sliding the canvas as a flat image.

Remaining visual gap: crater distribution and material mottling differ; the
interior wall still looks more constructed and less intricate than the reference;
fracture glow, dust, debris variety and optical scattering are not identical.
The reference's bright left surface has richer local contrast and irregular
luminous breakup. The new secondary journey has no source screenshot defining
its hidden chambers, so its design is authored rather than a recovered original.

## Gravity

The reference contains a large rippled mercury ceiling that recedes into haze,
uneven chunky hanging black masses with eroded broken bottoms, many small
background columns, a bright cratered floor, a terrain/grid boundary, a central
upward droplet column, oblique orbital ellipses, delicate callouts and a narrow
right telemetry rail. The build uses live geological meshes, displaced terrain,
reflected liquid, instanced droplets, projected nodes and the same broad layout.
The Ocean journey rolls the actual camera, presenting the suspended ceiling as a
reflective floor while leaving the liquid and field live.

Remaining visual gap: prominent slabs still have straighter faces/vertical facets
than the naturally eroded reference. Floor micro-erosion and exact crater shapes,
remote-rock distribution, cloud/fog structure, reflected wave patterns and subtle
luminance are not identical. Some instrumentation is smaller and mechanically
different. The new ocean/observation views are working authored extensions.

## Astrolabe

The reference is a thick tapering cratered crescent with narrow horns, a dark
nested eclipse with gold/violet limb emission, a dense dissolving triangular
purple/gold surface, several engraved instrument tracks and multiple oblique
orbits. Left chapter spine, right source/radar column and bottom waveform frame
the object. The build uses real sculpted crescent geometry, finite crater relief,
masked material dissolution, three independent rotating 3D ring groups, a live
corona and functional timeline. Guided ring studies reveal oblique geometry and
can capture the real postprocessed view.

Remaining visual gap: crater/erosion density, crescent cross-section, lower horn,
wire distribution, ring ornament and luminous density differ from the source.
The source has more crowded fine upper-right wire intersections and atmospheric
specks; the build is cleaner and less optically dense. Corona direction, star
field and spacing/microtype are not identical. Rendered image capture demonstrates
real geometry; it does not establish photographic equivalence.

The stills supply no original animation or audio. The motion and sound here are
authored interpretations using bounded real-time models. Exact realism, every
physical solver and full client acceptance are not claimed.
