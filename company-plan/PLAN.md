## Earn, deliver, establish runway

Next 90 days; proposed targets, not achieved results

### Cash & runway

Know what must be earned before each bill falls due.

- **List due bills** — Separate business costs, personal living costs and delivery obligations; record due dates. Evidence: A dated obligations ledger; no assumed cash. Timing: Days 1–2.
- **13-week cash view** — Forecast weekly receipts and payments; update from actual bank movements. Evidence: A rolling cash sheet with a visible shortfall. Timing: Day 2; update weekly.
- **Protect deposits** — Reserve unearned client money for its agreed delivery and refund obligations. Evidence: Available cash excludes restricted or unearned money. Timing: Every payment.
- **Pause new spend** — No new paid agents, subscriptions, advertising, hiring or Labs purchases without funded need. Evidence: New discretionary commitments stay at zero. Timing: Immediately.
- **Check urgent gap** — Match cash due in the next 7/30 days against genuinely collectible earned receipts. Evidence: Uncovered bills identified before their due date. Timing: Daily initially.
- **Discuss timing early** — Where needed, discuss legitimate payment timing with counterparties; do not assume concessions. Evidence: Any changed terms recorded and actually agreed. Timing: Before obligations fall due.
- **Cost each job** — Include delivery time, cloud, support, rework and payment costs before quoting. Evidence: A costed scope and achievable delivery plan. Timing: Before every proposal.
- **Build first buffer** — Retain earned surplus after obligations; first protect the next month of actual baseline costs. Evidence: Cash reserve measured against the current burn. Timing: After earned receipts.

### One paid offer

Sell one bounded outcome you can deliver using existing resources.

- **Choose buyer** — Pick one reachable buyer group with a costly, concrete technical problem. Evidence: One buyer profile supported by conversations. Timing: Week 1.
- **Choose outcome** — Select an automation pilot, performance fix or technical design service that matches real skills. Evidence: One clearly described deliverable. Timing: Week 1.
- **Bound the scope** — Specify inputs, exclusions, deliverables, review rounds and acceptance criteria. Evidence: A one-page scope. Timing: Week 1.
- **Use current hardware** — Choose work deliverable without purchasing equipment; client supplies required paid accounts. Evidence: No unfunded delivery dependency. Timing: Before offering.
- **Price from evidence** — Check value, actual effort and buyer response; model prices are not market validation. Evidence: Recorded pricing assumptions and feedback. Timing: Each proposal.
- **Offer paid discovery** — Where uncertainty is high, sell a useful standalone investigation before a larger build. Evidence: A defined discovery result, not a vague consultation. Timing: When scope is uncertain.
- **Sell a small pilot** — Offer a narrow first engagement with clear milestones and payment terms. Evidence: Proposed target: two paid pilots by day 90. Timing: Days 15–90.
- **Keep only what works** — Revise or stop an offer that fails buyer or delivery tests instead of expanding its scope. Evidence: A weekly keep/change/stop decision. Timing: Every week.

### Research practice

Make Labs a credible experimental organization with reproducible learning.

- **Set a falsifiable test** — State what observation would support or reject the thesis. Evidence: Predefined success and stop conditions. Timing: Before each experiment.
- **Record methods** — Version code, settings, datasets and constraints appropriately. Evidence: A repeatable experiment record. Timing: During research.
- **Respect data rights** — Use data and software with appropriate permission and controls. Evidence: Recorded provenance and access boundaries. Timing: Before use.
- **Publish real findings** — Include failures and limitations rather than only impressive demonstrations. Evidence: Evidence-backed research notes. Timing: At milestones.
- **Manage compute** — Set workload budgets and limits; measure cost per useful result. Evidence: Actual usage and spending records. Timing: Every run.
- **Review independently** — Seek relevant technical criticism and reproduce important results. Evidence: Issues and follow-up checks recorded. Timing: Before strong claims.
- **Transfer useful work** — Promote an experiment into a product or Studio method only when the use case is validated. Evidence: An explicit handover and maintenance owner. Timing: At a transfer gate.
- **Archive honestly** — Stop, document and preserve unproductive or superseded work. Evidence: Clear archive status and lessons. Timing: At stop decisions.
